What This Page Is

This is how to do the job — what you own, the procedures, the judgment calls, and the handful of things that aren't written down anywhere else. It is deliberately not a reference manual. Property details, reservations, vendor numbers, supply lists: those live in systems that stay current, and a copy here would only go stale and start lying to you.

The point is that you don't have to ask Kyle. If something here is wrong, missing, or you had to ask anyway — tell him. It gets fixed, and the next person doesn't hit it.
The job, in two questions: Are the guests happy? Are the units healthy? If both are genuinely yes, you're done for the day.
What You Own
  • Guest messaging — fully yours. Ask Kyle anything, any time.
  • Cleaner coordination — crew on site daily, early and late checkouts communicated to them, units clean on time.
  • Restocking and amenities — every item in all 12 units. We never run out.
  • Walkthroughs — every unit, every week.
  • Vendors — you dispatch and schedule. Always copy Kyle.
  • Damage claims — start to finish. 14-day clock.
  • Reviews — all of them, weekly, no approval needed. And you review the guests.
  • Ulises' monthly invoice — you verify the counts before Kyle pays it.
Cleaners clean. They don't repair and they don't restock. They'll help when they can and it's good of them, but it is never expected and never assumed. If a unit has no coffee, that's yours — whether we were out of stock or it just didn't get put back.

SOP — Reviews (Weekly)
All reviews are done in Hostaway. Same slot every week — respond to what came in, and review the guests who checked out.

You're not writing to the person who left it; they've gone home. You're writing to the next hundred people deciding between us and the place down the street.

  • 4–5 stars: thank them by name, pick up the one specific thing they mentioned, invite them back. Two or three sentences. Never the same reply twice — they stack up visibly.
  • Under 4: wait until you're not annoyed. Acknowledge the real part plainly. "We've since replaced it" is the most valuable sentence you can write — if it isn't fixed, go fix it, then reply. Correct facts calmly, once. Never argue, never sarcasm, never mention what they paid.
  • Review the guest too. Great ones: say so, it takes twenty seconds. Ones who broke something or snuck people in: factual, not angry. The next host relies on us being honest, same as we rely on them.
  • Anything fixable in a review goes into Notion Tasks. One guest bothered enough to write it down means ten who didn't.
Send it to Kyle first if they're asking for money, threatening legal action or a chargeback, alleging something unsafe, naming a neighbour or owner, or it's a Casitas review. Everything else, post it yourself.
Where it stands: since 1 September, 41 of 174 reviews have a reply — but every single one under 10 stars is answered, which is the right priority. The backlog is 133 five-star ones. Ten a week clears it without it ever feeling like a project.

Door Codes — How They Work
ChannelHow the code works
AirbnbAutomatic. Airbnb creates a code on the Schlage lock with the guest's name and dates, and shows it to them in the Airbnb app. You don't make these. You can see them in Schlage under Manage Access.
Vrbo & DirectManual. One shared code per property, always active, no dates. You change these — see below. The code reaches the guest in the automated Hostaway check-in message.
Because the direct code never expires, it's only as secure as how often we change it. Every past Vrbo and direct guest still knows the last one.
How To — Change the Direct / Vrbo Codes
How often: at least once a month across all properties, ideally two or three times a month. It's a manual job — nothing does it for you, and nothing reminds you. Put it in your calendar.
  1. Pick a new code for the property. Avoid anything guessable — not the street number, not 1234.
  2. Change it in the Schlage Home app — open the lock, Manage Access, edit the existing direct code. Don't add a second one; edit the one that's there or you'll end up with a lock full of live codes.
  3. Change it in Hostaway too — listing settings → additional info → policies. This step is the one that gets missed, and missing it means the automated check-in message sends guests a code that no longer opens the door.
  4. Check for guests already booked who were sent the old code in an earlier message. If someone's arriving in the next few days, message them the new one.
  5. Note the date you did it, so you know when it's due again.
Not every property is a Schlage lock. The Avila units and Hummingbird are. Flora Farm is on Emtek keypads — an older system, no app, codes are changed at the keypad itself. Casitas Estate uses physical keys in a lockbox — nothing to rotate, but the lockbox code is sent 24h before arrival, so check it actually went out.
Roughly 80% of our bookings are Airbnb, so a direct code might only get used once or twice between changes. That's fine — it isn't wasted effort, it's the reason the risk stays small.
How To — Check or Reset the Internet Remotely

Guest says the WiFi is slow or down. Most of the time this is fixable from your phone in two minutes, without anyone driving anywhere.

  1. Work out which network they're on. The 9 Avila compound units share one Google Nest mesh with extenders. Hummingbird has its own router (main bedroom closet). Flora and Casitas are separate again. A problem on the compound mesh affects nine units at once — a problem at Hummingbird affects one.
  2. Open the Google Home app and find the Avila network. You can see whether the mesh points are online, which are struggling, and how many devices are connected.
  3. If a point is offline or slow, restart it from the app. Give it 2–3 minutes to come back before telling the guest anything.
  4. Tell the guest something true while it reboots: "It can get slower on busy weekends — I'm resetting the router on our end now, give it a couple of minutes."
  5. If it doesn't come back, or a single unit is down while the others are fine, that's Cameron (WiFi/tech, in Notion Contacts). Text him and copy Kyle.
If you can't get into Google Home, ask Kyle to add you and walk you through it once. It's a two-minute setup and it's the difference between fixing this from your sofa and losing an evening to it.
Busy weekends genuinely do slow the compound mesh — nine units sharing one network. That's a real explanation, not an excuse, and guests take it fine when you're straight about it.

Generally no, but use judgment.

How It Works

Messages from Airbnb, Vrbo and direct bookings all land in Hostaway. You reply there and it syncs back. You never need to log into Airbnb or Vrbo to answer someone.

The whole point of your role: Kyle gets texted by one person instead of thirty guests every weekend. You're the filter — which doesn't mean you're on your own. Ask him anything, especially early on.
How To Talk To Guests
  • Be yourself — friendly, helpful, to the point. Two or three sentences is plenty.
  • Read what they actually said and answer that.
  • No "Dear", no sign-off, no "we apologize for the inconvenience". Just talk.
  • Contractions always. "Totally", "for sure", "definitely" are all fine.
  • If they're excited, match it. If they're frustrated, be honest and direct.
The 80% rule. If you're 80% sure, send it. An 80% right answer in ten minutes beats a perfect one in four hours. "Let us know if that doesn't sort it, but try this" covers most of troubleshooting. Use Claude to draft anything you're stuck on.

The 14-Day Clock
Airbnb damage claims must be filed within 14 days of the guest's checkout. Not from when you noticed. Not from when the cleaner mentioned it. And if another guest checks in before that, the window closes sooner.

Damage found on a Monday is worth money on Monday and worth nothing three weeks later, no matter how good the photos are. If you see damage, start the claim the same day — while you're still holding the phone with the pictures on it. You can always withdraw a request; you cannot reopen an expired one.

This has already cost us. A Palm House claim — all the sheets, a duvet, pillows, fully photographed — sat in a group chat until it expired. Everything needed to get paid existed. Nobody filed it.

Build the Evidence
  1. Photos. Wide shot showing which room, then close-ups. More than you think. Don't strip the timestamps.
  2. The "before". The cleaner's walkthrough video from the previous turnover proves it was fine when this guest arrived. This is the quiet reason those videos matter.
  3. The cost. A receipt, a screenshot of the replacement listing, or a written quote. A number with nothing behind it gets denied.
  4. The reservation. Guest name, property, exact dates, channel. From Hostaway.
  5. Who found it and when. "Judith, at the Tuesday turnover, around 11:30." Written down.
Tell the crew what you need: one wide shot showing which unit and room, not just a close-up of the stain. A close-up of a stained sheet could be any sheet in the portfolio.
Filing — Airbnb

About 80% of bookings, so this is the path you'll use most. It's the Resolution Center, under the reservation.

  1. Within 14 days of checkout, open the reservation and start a reimbursement request.
  2. Itemize. "Linens" is weak. "Queen fitted sheet, $34 — red wine, wouldn't wash out" is strong. Attach photos and the cost to each line.
  3. Keep it factual. What was damaged, when, what it costs. No apologising, no arguing. You're documenting, not persuading.
  4. The guest has 24 hours to pay, part-pay, decline or ignore.
  5. If they don't pay in full, escalate to Airbnb under Host damage protection. "They said no" is the halfway point, not the end.
  6. Log the outcome in Notion either way, and put it in your weekly note.
Reviews and claims collide. A guest just asked for money sometimes leaves a bad review, and the windows overlap. Don't let that stop you — the deadline is real. If a claim is big enough that the review risk matters, tell Kyle before you file.
