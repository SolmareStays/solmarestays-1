Property Walkthroughs

Weekly walkthroughs catch problems before guests do. Be thorough, be efficient, fix what you can on the spot.

What to Look For

▶
  • Point out EVERYTHING — burned-out lightbulb, scuff on wall, wobbly chair, slow drain, dusty fan blade
  • Take photos of anything that needs attention — send to Kyle or log in Notion
  • Check beds, bathrooms, kitchen, outdoor areas, parking signage
  • Check supplies — low on TP, towels, coffee, cleaning products?
  • Check AC/heat — working? Set correctly?
  • Check door codes — do they work?
  • Check for guest left-behinds from previous stays

Fix It If You Can

▶
  • Quick fix? Just do it — tighten a screw, replace a lightbulb, straighten a picture, wipe a surface
  • Don't spend 30 minutes on something — if more than a few minutes, log it and move on
  • Anything that needs a vendor — note it, tell Kyle, he'll dispatch
Real example: Kyle noticed a loose screw at Coral House during a walkthrough, said "I'll do it later," forgot, and had to make a separate 45-minute trip for a 30-second fix. Having it written down and doing it while you're there saves hours.

Be Efficient with Visits

▶
  • Batch by location — 9 Avila units are within a block, do them together
  • Casitas Estate + Flora Farm are both in Arroyo Grande — same trip
  • Monterey Heights (SLO) is standalone — combine with errands in town
  • 5-10 min for a studio, 10-15 for larger properties
  • High-turnover days (weekends), prioritize units with guests arriving that day
Always have Notion open during walkthroughs. The property page has the amenity list, open tasks, and details you won't memorize. You don't have to check every item, but scan it as a reference — it'll catch things your brain misses (like a missing bath mat).

Maintenance — Two Types

Non-Urgent: Batch for Handyman

▶

Cosmetic, annoying but livable. Doesn't block the stay.

  • Log it in Notion Tasks (property, issue, who reported, photos)
  • Compile into a list for the handyman's next visit
  • "Hey Zach, when you come by next week, I have a list of 8 things at the compound. Do as many as you can."
  • Examples: fire pit pilot button, loose cable on deck, scuff on wall, light bulb
Don't call a handyman out for one loose screw at $100/hr. Batch it. He'll take 30 seconds when he's already there for other stuff.

Urgent: Call Vendor NOW

▶

Blocks the stay or is a safety hazard. Needs same-day fix.

  • No hot water, broken toilet, active leak, safety issue, broken lock, pest emergency
  • Call the vendor directly (see Contacts tab). Give them Kyle's email to invoice.
  • Text Kyle with: property, issue, severity, what you did
  • If guest-reported mid-stay: "That's not right, let me get this sorted." Don't promise a timeline.

How It Works

All guest messages from Airbnb, Vrbo, and direct bookings flow into Hostaway. You respond from Hostaway and it syncs back to the guest's platform. You don't need to log into Airbnb or Vrbo to respond.

The whole point of this role: Kyle wants to get texted by ONE person (you) instead of 30 guests every weekend. You handle guest communication. You're never bothering Kyle — especially these first few months, reach out whenever you need to. But the goal is that you're the filter between guests and Kyle.
How to Talk to Guests

Just be yourself. You're texting someone who's staying at a place you take care of. Keep it short, keep it real, keep it warm. Don't overthink it.

The General Vibe

▶
  • Be you — friendly, helpful, to the point
  • Short messages. 2-3 sentences is plenty. Nobody wants a novel.
  • Read what they actually said and respond to that specific thing
  • If they're excited, be warm. If they're frustrated, be honest and direct.
  • Don't start with "Dear" or "Hello" — just talk
  • Don't end with a signature or sign-off — just stop when you're done
  • Don't say "we apologize for the inconvenience" or anything that sounds like a corporate email
  • Use contractions always (you're, it's, don't, we're)
  • "totally," "definitely," "for sure" are fine

The 80% Rule

▶

If you're 80% confident in the answer, send it. An 80% right answer in 10 minutes is better than a perfect answer in 4 hours. If you don't know, use Claude to help draft a response. If you're truly stuck, text Kyle.

"Hey, let us know if you're still having problems, but try this." — That covers most troubleshooting situations. You don't need to know every detail about every unit.

Emergency Procedures
Life-threatening: Call 911 first. Then text Kyle immediately.

Guest Lockout

▶
  1. Open the Schlage app and unlock the door remotely. Get them in the unit first — that's the priority, whether it's 2 AM or 2 PM.
  2. Check the code — is it active? Is it the right lock? Did it sync? Try resending it.
  3. If smart lock won't work: direct them to the emergency lockbox (beam by front door, code 0000 for compound properties).
  4. If lockbox fails too, text Kyle. He may need to go on-site or coordinate.
  5. On-site visit = $50/hr call-out rate for you.

  3. Text Kyle with: property, issue, severity, what you did.
  4. To guest: "That's not right, let me get this sorted." Never promise a timeline.
  5. On-site visit = $50/hr call-out rate.
