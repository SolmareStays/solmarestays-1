What This Page Is

This is how to do the job — what you own, the procedures, the judgment calls, and the handful of things that aren't written down anywhere else. It is deliberately not a reference manual. Property details, reservations, vendor numbers, supply lists: those live in systems that stay current, and a copy here would only go stale and start lying to you.

The point is that you don't have to ask Kyle. If something here is wrong, missing, or you had to ask anyway — tell him. It gets fixed, and the next person doesn't hit it.
The job, in two questions: Are the guests happy? Are the units healthy? If both are genuinely yes, you're done for the day.

The Shape Of The Job

Most of this role is the same handful of things on a loop: know what's happening today, keep the units turning, walk them weekly, keep the shelves full, and keep Kyle unsurprised. The daily stuff is reactive — messages, turnovers, the odd fire. The weekly stuff is the part that actually keeps the portfolio healthy, and it's the part that quietly slides if you let it.

If you only protect three things in a week: every unit walked, every turnover closed in Hostaway the same day, and the restock board shrinking. Everything else recovers on its own. Those three don't.

★ SOP — Turnovers Focus area

The cleans are happening — it's the recording that slipped. Marking the Hostaway task complete is what sends the guest their "your place is ready" message, so an unmarked clean is an unready unit as far as they know. Closing every turnover the same day is the clearest win you have.

  1. Confirm the crew is on site and tell them about any early or late checkout before they arrive.
  2. Cleaner marks the task inProgress on arrival.
  3. Walkthrough video + "[Unit] — cleaned" to the crew chat.
  4. Task marked completed in Hostaway. This is the step that slips.
  5. You sweep twice — 2:45 PM and end of day — comparing chat against Hostaway, closing anything finished.
Hostaway is the record. The group chat is not. "Done" in the chat changes nothing in the system.
The clock: 29 hours from checkout for 11 of the 12. Casitas is the exception — it runs to Thursday 3 PM instead, because six buildings don't turn in a day.

Same-day turnovers are the ones that bite. There's no slack in them at all: the guest leaves at 11 and the next arrives at 3. Check every unit with a check-in around 2:30, and if one isn't ready 30 minutes before check-in, that's yours to chase — call the cleaner, get a real ETA, and tell the guest before they arrive to a locked or dirty unit. A guest who's been warned is annoyed; a guest who's surprised leaves a review about it.
Same-day or not at all. Our worst lag since September was 19 days, and it started as "I'll do it tonight." After a day nobody can remember whether it was really finished, so it sits.
★ SOP — Weekly Walkthroughs Non-negotiable
Every unit, every week. All 12. One day or spread across three — doesn't matter. The only way a unit gets skipped is if Kyle says so.
Walk them dirty, clean or empty — whatever state you find them in. A dirty unit is the most honest one: you see what guests actually did to it, what broke, and what the clean is about to hide. Give those the most time.

SOP — Reviews (Weekly)
All reviews are done in Hostaway. Same slot every week — respond to the reviews guests have left us. You're responding to their reviews, not writing reviews of them. Reviews of guests are written automatically by Hostaway — you never write or edit them.

You're not writing to the person who left it; they've gone home. You're writing to the next hundred people deciding between us and the place down the street.

  • 4–5 stars: thank them by name, pick up the one specific thing they mentioned, invite them back. Two or three sentences. Never the same reply twice — they stack up visibly.
  • Under 4: wait until you're not annoyed. Acknowledge the real part plainly. "We've since replaced it" is the most valuable sentence you can write — if it isn't fixed, go fix it, then reply. Correct facts calmly, once. Never argue, never sarcasm, never mention what they paid.
  • Anything fixable in a review goes into Notion Tasks. One guest bothered enough to write it down means ten who didn't.
  • The same complaint twice is a project, not a task. Flag it to Kyle.
  • Private feedback never goes public. Don’t quote or refer to what a guest said privately in a public response.
Every review is yours. Every platform, every property, Casitas included — no exceptions, nothing to send anyone first. Post it yourself.

Door Codes — How They Work
ChannelHow the code works
AirbnbAutomatic. Airbnb creates a code on the Schlage lock with the guest's name and dates, and shows it to them in the Airbnb app. You don't make these. You can see them in Schlage under Manage Access.
Vrbo & DirectManual. One shared code per property, always active, no dates. You change these — see below. The code reaches the guest in the automated Hostaway check-in message.
Because the direct code never expires, it's only as secure as how often we change it. Every past Vrbo and direct guest still knows the last one.
How To — Change the Direct / Vrbo Codes
How often: at least once a month across all properties, aim for twice a month. This is yours. It's a manual job — nothing does it for you, and nothing reminds you. Put it in your calendar.
  1. Pick a new code for the property. Avoid anything guessable — not the street number, not 1234.
  2. Change it in the Schlage Home app — open the lock, Manage Access, edit the existing direct code. Don't add a second one; edit the one that's there or you'll end up with a lock full of live codes.
  3. Change it in Hostaway too — listing settings → additional info → policies. This step is the one that gets missed, and missing it means the automated check-in message sends guests a code that no longer opens the door.
  4. Check anyone already mid-stay. Guests get their code in the automated check-in message on the morning of arrival, so anyone arriving tomorrow gets the new one automatically — but someone already in the unit is holding the old code. Message them the new one directly.
  5. Note the date you did it, so you know when it's due again.
Not every property is a Schlage lock. The Avila units and Hummingbird are. Flora Farm is on Emtek keypads — an older system, no app, codes are changed at the keypad itself. Casitas Estate uses physical keys in a lockbox — nothing to rotate, but the lockbox code is sent 24h before arrival, so check it actually went out.
Roughly 80% of our bookings are Airbnb, so a direct code might only get used once or twice between changes. That's fine — it isn't wasted effort, it's the reason the risk stays small.
How To — WiFi Problems
Our WiFi runs on UniFi. You can get into the Avila compound network remotely and actually see what's going on. The other properties have no remote access — for those you're diagnosing by question, not by dashboard.

Work through it in this order. Most of it you can do from your phone.

  1. How many units are affected? The nine Avila compound units share one network — if several guests complain at once, it's the network. One guest alone is almost always local to that unit.
  2. Avila? Open UniFi and look. You can see whether access points are online and what's connected. If something's down or struggling, restart it from there and give it 2–3 minutes.
  3. Check Spectrum for an outage. If the service is down in the area, there's nothing to fix at our end — and that's genuinely useful to know, because it changes what you tell the guest and stops you sending anyone out for nothing.
  4. No outage, and only one unit? It's almost certainly local — a router or access point in that unit. Ask them to unplug it for 30 seconds and plug it back in. That fixes most of these.
  5. Still down after that — that's Cameron (WiFi/tech, Notion Contacts). Text him and copy Kyle.
What to say meanwhile: "Let me take a look at it on our end — give me a couple of minutes." If it's a Spectrum outage, tell them plainly: guests are completely fine with "the provider has an outage in the area, here's what we know." They're not fine with silence.
Busy weekends genuinely do slow the compound — nine units on one network. That's a real explanation and worth saying, but check UniFi before you reach for it, or you'll use it on a night something is actually broken.
If you don't have UniFi access yet, get Kyle to set you up and walk it once. It's the difference between fixing this from your sofa and losing an evening to it.

How To — Late Checkout

Rare. The default is no — but for a good guest, it’s your call.

How It Works

Messages from Airbnb, Vrbo and direct bookings all land in Hostaway. You reply there and it syncs back. You never need to log into Airbnb or Vrbo to answer someone.

The whole point of your role: Kyle gets texted by one person instead of thirty guests every weekend. You're the filter — which doesn't mean you're on your own. Ask him anything, especially early on.
How To Talk To Guests
  • Be nice and hospitable — really friendly. That comes first, every message.
  • Ask guests what they want. Before you decide an answer, find out what would actually make their stay work.
  • Be yourself. Short is fine; curt never is.
  • Read what they actually said and answer that.
  • No "Dear", no sign-off, no "we apologize for the inconvenience". Just talk.
  • Contractions always. "Totally", "for sure", "definitely" are all fine.
  • If they're excited, match it. If they're frustrated, stay warm, be honest, and own it.
The 80% rule. If you're 80% sure, send it. An 80% right answer in ten minutes beats a perfect one in four hours. "Let us know if that doesn't sort it, but try this" covers most of troubleshooting. Use Claude to draft anything you're stuck on — and for anything sensitive, or when you’re unsure, ask Kyle for paste-ready wording.

Common Situations
"The WiFi is slow / down"
Check it from your phone first — see How-Tos → WiFi Problems. Password is in Hostaway and their guidebook. Busy weekends can slow the compound, but check UniFi before you say so.
"Do you have AC?"
Check the unit in Hostaway before answering — it varies, and some units have heat but no AC. If it's a no: "No AC in this one, but the evenings cool right down and there are ceiling fans and good cross-ventilation. Most guests are totally comfortable."
"Can we bring our dog?"
Only some units are pet-friendly and the fee differs — check Hostaway for that listing. If theirs isn't: "Not pet-friendly unfortunately, but we've got a couple that are — want me to check availability?" Casitas needs prior approval, so escalate.
"I can't find my door code"
Airbnb guest: it's in the Airbnb app under their trip, and visible to you in Schlage. Vrbo or direct: it's in their Hostaway check-in message. Send it directly either way.
Something's broken mid-stay
"That's not right — let me get it sorted." Never promise a timeline. If it blocks the stay, call the vendor now (Notion Contacts) and copy Kyle. If it's cosmetic, log it in Notion Tasks for the next maintenance window.
"We found bugs" / pest report
Yours. Never downplay it — "Really sorry, let me get this handled right now." Get the pest vendor out (Notion Contacts), copy Kyle on the thread. Tell Kyle straight away too, because he may want to offer something, and that part is his.
"The bed is uncomfortable"
Yours. Every unit has the same mattress, so there's nothing to swap — but don't say "they're all the same" either. Offer what you can: an extra mattress topper, more pillows, a different room if there is one. If they push toward a refund, that's where Kyle comes in.
Guest wants to cancel or change dates
Yours now. Check the calendar and the booking's cancellation terms in Hostaway and handle the modification. If it involves money moving back to them, stop and get Kyle — the change is yours, the refund is his.
Guest is upset — properly upset
Yours to hold. Acknowledge fast and honestly: "That's not right, let me get this sorted." Don't get defensive, don't over-explain, don't promise a timeline you can't keep. Fix what can be fixed and tell Kyle what happened. The only line you don't cross is money — the moment they ask for a refund or compensation, that's Kyle's.
Guest left something behind
Ask the crew. If found: "We found it! Happy to post it — send us an address. Shipping would be on you." Crew should always set aside found items, never bin them.
Guest wants to phone you
"We're quickest on messages — what can I help with?" If they insist, you can call them — or hand it to Kyle if you’d rather.
Cleaner can't make it, or a guest won't leave
Both are yours first — loop Kyle in fast. A missed cleaner on a same-day turnover is a fire drill: find cover, then tell him. A guest still there at 11:15 — nudge them kindly and keep the crew moving; if they don’t budge, bring Kyle in.
The 14-Day Clock
Airbnb damage claims must be filed within 14 days of the guest's checkout. Not from when you noticed. Not from when the cleaner mentioned it. Document it before the next guest checks in, while the evidence is still there.

Damage found on a Monday is worth money on Monday and worth nothing three weeks later, no matter how good the photos are. If you see damage, start the claim the same day — while you're still holding the phone with the pictures on it. You can always withdraw a request; you cannot reopen an expired one.

This has already cost us. A Palm House claim — all the sheets, a duvet, pillows, fully photographed — sat in a group chat until it expired. Everything needed to get paid existed. Nobody filed it.

Build the Evidence
  1. Photos. Wide shot showing which room, then close-ups. More than you think. Don't strip the timestamps.
  2. The "before". The cleaner's walkthrough video from the previous turnover proves it was fine when this guest arrived. This is the quiet reason those videos matter.
  3. The cost. A receipt, a screenshot of the replacement listing, or a written quote. A number with nothing behind it gets denied.
  4. The reservation. Guest name, property, exact dates, channel. From Hostaway.
  5. Who found it and when. "Judith, at the Tuesday turnover, around 11:30." Written down.
Tell the crew what you need: one wide shot showing which unit and room, not just a close-up of the stain. A close-up of a stained sheet could be any sheet in the portfolio.
Filing — Airbnb

About 80% of bookings, so this is the path you'll use most. It's the Resolution Center, under the reservation.

  1. Within 14 days of checkout, open the reservation and start a reimbursement request.
  2. Itemize. "Linens" is weak. "Queen fitted sheet, $34 — red wine, wouldn't wash out" is strong. Attach photos and the cost to each line.
  3. Keep it factual. What was damaged, when, what it costs. No apologising, no arguing. You're documenting, not persuading.
  4. The guest has 24 hours to pay, part-pay, decline or ignore.
  5. If they don't pay in full, escalate to Airbnb under Host damage protection. "They said no" is the halfway point, not the end.
  6. Log the outcome in Notion either way, and put it in your weekly note.
Reviews and claims collide. A guest just asked for money sometimes leaves a bad review, and the windows overlap. Don't let that stop you — the deadline is real. If a claim is big enough that the review risk matters, tell Kyle before you file.

Stains — the Everyday Case
  1. Crew photographs the item at the turnover and posts it to the crew chat
  2. You pull it out of the chat that day — a photo in a group chat is not a record, it scrolls away
  3. Does it wash out, or is the item dead? If Judith couldn't shift it, it's dead.
  4. Dead: replacement cost + photos + reservation → file it
  5. Replace the item first so the unit isn't short for the next guest. Paperwork second.
Yours vs Kyle's
Rule of thumb: money coming in from a guest who broke something is yours to chase. Money going out to a guest — refunds, discounts, goodwill — is always Kyle's.

Straight to Kyle: anything that becomes a negotiation, anything where they're asking for a refund and we're asking them for money, direct-booking recovery, four-figure damage, insurance, legal threats, anything involving an owner, and letting anything $100 or more go for goodwill. Under $100, the thresholds above are your call.

Scheduling & Invoices
  • Open the Hostaway calendar before you give anyone a time. Never book into an occupied unit without asking the guest.
  • Best window is a turnover day, 11 AM – 2 PM — guest gone, cleaner there to let them in, done before check-in.
  • Tell the crew chat whenever a vendor will be on site during a turnover, so parking works out.
  • Work mid-stay? Tell the guest before the vendor knocks. Never let a stranger turn up at a door unannounced.

Batch the small stuff. Keep a running handyman list; when it hits six or eight items, book the whole lot. A call-out for one loose screw is the easiest way to burn owner money — but so is a second trip, so if you're standing there and it takes two minutes, just do it.
